## Road Check — Day 2 (verified Sep 9, 2026)

Sources: [NPS Olympic Alerts & Conditions](https://www.nps.gov/olym/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) (page last updated Aug 27, 2026) and [WSDOT](https://wsdot.wa.gov/about/news/2026/long-weekend-and-overnight-closures-horizon-us-101-hoh-river-bridge-south-forks) (published Sep 2, 2026).

### All stops are paved-highway access — no gravel roads

| Stop | Access | Surface |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lake Crescent | US-101 along the south shore; short spur to Barnes Point / lodge | Paved |
| Ruby Beach | Short paved spur off US-101, paved lot | Paved (steep dirt **trail** down to the sand) |
| Kalaloch Tree of Life | Kalaloch Campground access off US-101 | Paved |
| World's largest Sitka spruce | **South Shore Rd, mile 3.3**, just east of Rain Forest Resort Village | Paved road; gravel parking lot; flat 0.3 mi gravel trail (~5 min) |
| Aberdeen Supercharger | US-101 → city streets | Paved |
| Aberdeen → Bellevue | US-12 → I-5 | Highway |

> [!warning] Don't overshoot at Quinault
> **South Shore Rd is closed at mile 8** (road washout). The spruce at mile 3.3 is well inside the open section, but this breaks the Quinault loop drive — you cannot continue around the lake. Turn around and return to US-101 the way you came. Also avoid **North Fork Rd** and **Graves Creek Rd** (both narrow gravel) — neither is on the route.

### US-101 Hoh River Bridge — on the route, open but slow

The bridge sits on US-101 between Forks and Ruby Beach, so Day 2 crosses it. Status:

- **Open on Sept 9–10.** Closures don't begin until Sept 14.
- **One-way alternating traffic at 25 mph since Jul 14, 2026**, controlled by temporary signals on both ends. Budget extra time in the 11:05 AM Ruby Beach leg — the itinerary's 1h30m Sequim→Ruby Beach estimate does not account for a signal wait.
- **There is no detour across the Hoh River.** Going around the east side of the peninsula adds 4+ hours.
- Upcoming full closures, for reference if the trip slips: nightly 10:30 PM–5:30 AM Sept 14–22, then all-day Sept 24–29, Oct 1–6, and Oct 8–13.

### Other closures — not on the route

- **Mora Rd** closed to all traffic Jul 8–Oct 15 (Rialto Beach unreachable). NPS explicitly suggests Ruby Beach as the substitute, which is what you're already doing.
- **Elwha / Olympic Hot Springs Rd** closed at Madison Falls (washout).
- **Hoh Rain Forest** is open, but you're skipping it — the 2-hour entrance queue note still holds.

### Day 1 correction

NPS now lists **Obstruction Point Rd as open** (still gravel, still not for RVs/trailers). The note below says to avoid it — that remains a reasonable call for the car, but it isn't closed. More importantly: **there is no potable water at Hurricane Ridge** during the 2026 utility construction, and the parking lot work runs through August with possible rolling closures. Carry all your water up.

## Trail Notes — what each one actually gives you

Perks and trade-offs for every walk on the itinerary, so the optional ones can be swapped in or dropped on the day without re-researching.

### Day 1 — Hurricane Ridge

| Trail | Numbers | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hurricane Hill** | 3.2 mi RT · ~700 ft · paved | The marquee one. A genuine 360° summit: Mount Olympus and the Bailey Range across the valley to the south, and on a clear day the Strait of Juan de Fuca, Vancouver Island, and Mount Baker to the north. Paved end to end, so it's a big-view summit you can do in trail runners. Also the busiest trail on the ridge — the 12:45 PM start puts you behind the morning wave. |
| **Cirque Rim + Big Meadow** | ~1 mi total · flat · paved | Best wildlife-per-step of the whole trip. Olympic marmots — a species found nowhere else on earth — and black-tailed deer graze right beside the pavement, largely indifferent to people. Interpretive signs, wheelchair accessible, and Big Meadow opens onto the full valley panorama. The highest payoff for the least effort on Day 1. |
| **Sunrise Point** *(optional)* | ~1.5 mi RT · rolling | Solitude. Nearly everyone stops at the visitor-center loops, so a few hundred yards buys you ridge views without the crowd. Faces east and north, which makes it the better late-afternoon light of the two options. |
| **Switchback Trail** *(optional alt)* | 0.8 mi · steep · ~700 ft | The most view per mile on the ridge — it gains the Klahhane Ridge crest in well under a mile. Steep and fully exposed, with no shade and no water. Only worth it if Hurricane Hill left something in the legs. |

### Day 2 — Lake Crescent to Quinault

| Stop | Numbers | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Marymere Falls** | 1.8 mi RT · ~450 ft | A 90-ft falls at the back of deep old-growth — Douglas fir, western hemlock, cedar — with two wooden footbridges over Barnes Creek. Flat and easy for the first 0.9 mi, then a short steep switchback to two viewing platforms. Shaded the entire way, which matters more than it sounds after a day on the exposed ridge. |
| **Devil's Punchbowl** | ~5 min walk | Lake Crescent is unusually low in nitrogen, so almost no algae grows in it — the water is startlingly clear and an improbable turquoise. The arched footbridge over the cove is the shot. |
| **Ruby Beach** | short steep descent | Sea stacks offshore, huge bleached driftwood piles, and Cedar Creek cutting across the sand to the surf. Named for the garnet crystals that tint the sand reddish in places. The most photogenic beach on this stretch of coast, and the last unhurried stop before lunch. |
| **Beach 4** | short trail + beach walk | The best tidepooling on the Kalaloch strip — anemones, ochre sea stars, urchins in the rock shelves. Quieter than Ruby Beach because it takes slightly more effort to reach the pools. |
| **Kalaloch Tree of Life** | no walking | A Sitka spruce suspended over a hollowed-out gully, roots bridging the gap with essentially no soil beneath it. Structurally it should not still be there, which is the whole appeal. Right off the bluff by the campground. |
| **World's largest Sitka spruce** | 0.3 mi · flat gravel | 191 ft tall, 58 ft 11 in around, ~1,000 years old, with a 96-ft crown spread. Five flat minutes from the car for a tree that genuinely does not photograph. |
| **Merriman Falls** | roadside | A mossy tiered falls a few feet off South Shore Rd. Costs nothing, and almost nobody pulls over for it. |

> [!warning] Beach 4 depends on the tide
> The tide pools are only exposed around low tide — at high water there is nothing to see and the rock shelves are unsafe. Check the Kalaloch tide table against the 3:10 PM slot before committing.

### Beach 4 is also the schedule lever

It is the only stop on Day 2 that is both optional and expensive, which makes it the one to cut when the day runs long:

| Day 2 as planned | Home ~9:25 PM |
| --- | --- |
| Drop Beach 4 | Home ~8:50 PM |

Everything else is either a short walk, a roadside pull-off, or directly on the route, so cutting anything else saves minutes rather than half an hour. If the tide is wrong *or* the evening is running late, this is the clean drop — and both conditions tend to be known by the time you reach Kalaloch.

## Food & Supplies

Day 2 crosses a genuine food desert. Between Forks and Amanda Park the only real options are the two park lodges, and lunch service at both ends mid-afternoon — hence the reservation.

### Day 2 lunch — Kalaloch

| | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Creekside Restaurant** | Kalaloch Lodge, 157151 US-101 · (360) 962-2271 · [site](https://www.thekalalochlodge.com/dine-and-shop/creekside-restaurant/) · [OpenTable](https://www.opentable.com/r/creekside-kalaloch) |
| Lunch service | 11:30 AM – 2:30/3:00 PM daily (seasonal — confirm by phone) |
| Why here | Ocean view from the bluff, and it sits at the Kalaloch stop already on the route, so a sit-down lunch costs no extra driving. The Tree of Life is a 5-minute walk from the door. |
| Risk | Arriving 1:45 PM leaves little margin against a 2:30 close, and reservations are strongly recommended in September. |

**Bail-out:** you pass through **Forks around 12:25 PM**, an hour before the Kalaloch booking. If the Hoh River Bridge signal has eaten the schedule, eat in Forks instead — **Sully's Drive-In** at the north end of town is counter service straight off US-101, no wait, no reservation. Forks is the last decision point; after it there is nothing until Quinault.

Other Forks options if you want a table: Blakeslee's Bar & Grill (pub food), Pacific Pizza, Golden Gate Chinese (has vegan dishes).

### What it means for the plan

- **Dry both days.** Precip chance tops out at 12% anywhere on the route, and Thursday is 0–2% across the whole Day 2 loop. No rain gear needed beyond a shell.
- **Hurricane Ridge runs ~11°F colder than Sequim on Day 1 and ~15°F colder on Day 2**, and it's mostly sunny both days — the hike window is close to ideal. Wind is calm (2–7 mph), so no wind chill on the exposed ridge.
- **Layers still matter up top.** 63°F in sun at 5,000 ft with Thursday's daytime high only 54°F and falling through the afternoon; Thursday night on the ridge drops to 38°F.
- **The coast is the cool, grey part of Day 2.** Ruby Beach and Kalaloch sit at 59°F, mostly cloudy, with NW wind 9–14 mph off the water. Bring a windbreaker for the beach stops specifically — it will feel much colder than Lake Quinault (66°F) an hour later.
- **Patchy fog forecast for Sequim on Thursday night** — irrelevant to this itinerary since you're back in Bellevue, but worth knowing if plans slip a day.
- **No smoke or haze in any forecast**, which is not a given for September on the peninsula.
